# Used Car Buying Checklist

**Your complete guide to buying your first car, from research to drive-home day.**

---

> **How to use this document:**
> - Check off each item as you complete it
> - Work through the sections in order
> - Don't skip the "Red Flags" section — it could save you thousands

---

## 1. Before You Shop

### 1.1 Set Your Budget

Total budget for the car (not just the sticker price):

| Item | Estimated Cost |
|------|---------------|
| Purchase price | $ |
| Sales tax (varies by state) | $ |
| Title + registration | $ |
| Insurance (first 6 months) | $ |
| Any immediate repairs | $ |
| **Total** | **$** |

- [ ] Determine the maximum you can spend (including everything above)
- [ ] If financing, set a monthly payment you can afford
- [ ] Have your down payment saved
- [ ] Keep your emergency fund intact — don't drain it for the car

### 1.2 Get Pre-Approved for Financing

- [ ] Check your credit score (free at annualcreditreport.com)
- [ ] Get pre-approved at your bank or credit union **before** going to a dealer
- [ ] Compare rates from at least 2–3 lenders
- [ ] Know your debt-to-income ratio

> **Why this matters:** A pre-approval gives you leverage at the dealer and prevents you from getting stuck with a high-interest loan on the spot.

### 1.3 Get Insurance Quotes

- [ ] Call your insurance agent or get online quotes
- [ ] Get quotes for 2–3 cars you're considering
- [ ] Compare insurance costs across models

> **Heads up:** Insurance on used cars varies wildly by model. Some compact cars cost 2–3x more to insure than others. Check this **before** you fall in love with a car.

## 2. Research (Before You Even Look at Cars)

### 2.1 Know What You're Buying

- [ ] Look up the car's reliability ratings (Consumer Reports, J.D. Power)
- [ ] Check for common problems with that specific year/make/model
- [ ] Look up the car's safety ratings (NHTSA, IIHS)
- [ ] Check for open recalls at [nhtsa.gov/recalls](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls)
- [ ] Look up the car's market value (KBB, Edmunds, CarGurus)
- [ ] Research the car's maintenance schedule and typical costs

### 2.2 Check the Vehicle History

- [ ] Get the **VIN** from the seller before you go look at it
- [ ] Run a free VIN check at [nhtsa.gov](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) (recalls, complaints)
- [ ] Pay for a vehicle history report (Carfax or AutoCheck — ~$40)
- [ ] Look for: accidents, title issues, odometer rollback, flood damage
- [ ] Verify the VIN on the report matches the car in person

## 3. At the Seller

### 3.1 Before You Even Look at the Car

- [ ] Ask for the VIN over text/email before you drive there
- [ ] Run the VIN check before you leave the house
- [ ] Ask: **Why are you selling?**
- [ ] Ask: **How long have you owned it?**
- [ ] Ask: **Are there any known problems?**
- [ ] Ask: **Do you have maintenance records?**

### 3.2 Exterior Inspection — Walk Around the Car

**Paint and Body**
- [ ] Walk around the entire car at different angles and in different light
- [ ] Look for mismatched paint (sign of body work)
- [ ] Check panel gaps — should be even, not wider on one side
- [ ] Look for rust on wheel wells, rocker panels, bottom of doors
- [ ] Check for dents, scratches, and repainted areas
- [ ] Look for overspray on trim, lights, or rubber seals

**Tires**
- [ ] All four tires should match (brand, model, and tread depth)
- [ ] Check tread depth — insert a penny head-first; if you can see all of Lincoln's head, the tires are worn
- [ ] Look for uneven wear (alignment or suspension issues)
- [ ] Check tire date code (DOT number on sidewall) — tires over 6 years old need replacing
- [ ] Check the spare tire and jack

**Glass and Lights**
- [ ] Check all windows for chips or cracks
- [ ] Turn on all lights: headlights, brake lights, turn signals, hazards
- [ ] Check for condensation inside headlights or tail lights (seal failure)

### 3.3 Under the Hood (Engine Off)

- [ ] Check oil level and color — should be amber or black, **not milky** (milky = coolant leak)
- [ ] Check coolant level and color — not rusty
- [ ] Check brake fluid level
- [ ] Check power steering fluid
- [ ] Check transmission fluid — should be pink or red, **not brown** (brown = old or burned)
- [ ] Look for leaks under the car (puddles on the ground)
- [ ] Check battery terminals for corrosion (white/green buildup)
- [ ] Look for cracked or frayed belts
- [ ] Check for rodent damage (chewed wires, nests)

### 3.4 Interior Inspection

**Seats and Upholstery**
- [ ] Check for tears, stains, and burns
- [ ] Test all seat adjustments (manual and power)
- [ ] Pull seat belts all the way out — check for fraying
- [ ] Check floor mats for water damage or mildew smell

**Dashboard and Controls**
- [ ] Turn the key to ON (don't start) — all warning lights should illuminate
- [ ] Start the car — warning lights should turn off (except seatbelt)
- [ ] Check odometer — does it match the listing?
- [ ] Test AC — should get cold within 2–3 minutes
- [ ] Test heat — should get hot within 2–3 minutes
- [ ] Test all windows (up and down)
- [ ] Test power locks
- [ ] Test mirrors (adjust all of them)
- [ ] Test windshield wipers (all speeds + washer fluid)
- [ ] Test horn
- [ ] Test radio / infotainment
- [ ] Check USB ports and aux input
- [ ] Test Bluetooth pairing

**Trunk**
- [ ] Open and close trunk
- [ ] Check for spare tire, jack, and tools
- [ ] Look for signs of water damage or rust
- [ ] Check trunk floor for sagging or damage

### 3.5 Test Drive

**Before Driving**
- [ ] Adjust seat, mirrors, and steering wheel
- [ ] Buckle up
- [ ] Note the mileage

**Starting and Idling**
- [ ] Start the car — should start within 2–3 seconds
- [ ] Listen for unusual noises (knocking, ticking, grinding)
- [ ] Idle for 1–2 minutes — should idle smoothly
- [ ] Check exhaust smoke:

| Smoke Color | What It Means | Verdict |
|-------------|---------------|---------|
| None or light gray | Normal | OK |
| Blue | Burning oil | Problem |
| White (persistent) | Coolant leak | Problem |
| Black | Running rich | Check engine |

**Driving**
- [ ] Accelerate firmly — should be smooth, no hesitation
- [ ] Brake firmly from 40 mph — should stop straight, no pulling
- [ ] Drive over bumps — listen for clunking or rattling
- [ ] Turn steering wheel lock-to-lock — should be smooth
- [ ] Drive at highway speeds (55+ mph) — listen for wind noise
- [ ] Check for vibrations at speed
- [ ] Put it in reverse — should engage smoothly
- [ ] Try cruise control if equipped

**After Driving**
- [ ] Park and let it idle for a minute
- [ ] Check for new puddles under the car
- [ ] Smell for burning or sweet odors

## 4. Questions to Ask the Seller

- [ ] Why are you selling?
- [ ] How long have you owned it?
- [ ] Are there any known problems?
- [ ] Has it been in any accidents?
- [ ] Do you have maintenance records?
- [ ] Has anything been replaced or repaired recently?
- [ ] Is the title clean? (No salvage, rebuilt, or flood branding)
- [ ] Are you the title holder? (Name on title must match their ID)
- [ ] Is there a loan on it? (Must have lien release from the bank)
- [ ] How many owners has it had?
- [ ] Do you have the second key?
- [ ] Has it passed emissions recently?

## 5. Pre-Purchase Inspection (PPI)

This is the most important step. **Do not skip it.**

- [ ] Find a trusted mechanic **before** you buy
- [ ] Independent mechanic or dealership service center — either works
- [ ] Cost: $100–200 (worth every penny)
- [ ] They check: engine, transmission, suspension, brakes, electrical, frame
- [ ] Get a **written report** with findings
- [ ] Use findings to negotiate the price — or walk away

> **Where to find a mechanic:** Ask friends, check Google reviews, look for ASE-certified shops. Call ahead and ask if they do pre-purchase inspections.

## 6. The Paperwork

### 6.1 Before You Sign

- [ ] VIN on the title matches the VIN on the car (dashboard + door jamb)
- [ ] Name on the title matches the seller's ID
- [ ] Title is clean (no salvage, rebuilt, flood, or liens)
- [ ] Odometer reading on title matches the car's odometer
- [ ] Bill of sale includes: date, price, VIN, buyer and seller info
- [ ] Get copies of **everything**

### 6.2 Title Transfer

- [ ] Seller signs the title over to you
- [ ] Remove the seller's plates
- [ ] You need: title, bill of sale, your ID, proof of insurance
- [ ] Register the car at your local DMV / MVD
- [ ] Get temporary tags if needed

### 6.3 Registration and Fees

- [ ] Title fee
- [ ] Registration fee
- [ ] Emissions test (if required in your state — Arizona requires it in most counties)
- [ ] Sales tax (varies by state)

### 6.4 Insurance

- [ ] Add the car to your insurance policy **before** you drive it home
- [ ] Get proof of insurance (required to register)

## 7. Red Flags — Walk Away

Stop and leave if you see any of these:

| Red Flag | Why It's a Problem |
|----------|-------------------|
| Seller won't let you get a PPI | They're hiding something |
| Seller won't show you the title | Could be stolen or have liens |
| Title is in someone else's name | Could be curbstoning or title jumping |
| VIN on car doesn't match title | Could be stolen or cloned |
| Odometer seems tampered with | Illegal and dishonest |
| Seller is rushing you to decide | High-pressure = bad deal |
| Price is too good to be true | It probably is |
| Seller only accepts cash, won't meet publicly | Safety risk + scam potential |
| Salvage or rebuilt title | Only buy if you fully understand the risks |
| Seller has multiple cars for sale | Curbstoner (unlicensed dealer flipping) |

## 8. After You Buy

- [ ] Change the oil and filter (even if the seller says it was just done)
- [ ] Check all fluids
- [ ] Check tire pressure
- [ ] Register the car at DMV
- [ ] Get license plates
- [ ] Update your insurance
- [ ] Keep all receipts for repairs and maintenance
- [ ] Set reminders for future maintenance (oil changes, tire rotations)

## Quick Reference: What Good vs. Bad Looks Like

| Item | Good | Bad |
|------|------|-----|
| Oil | Amber to black | Milky (coolant leak) |
| Transmission fluid | Pink/red | Brown/burnt smell |
| Exhaust | None or light gray | Blue, persistent white, or black |
| Tires | Even tread, matching set | Uneven wear, different brands |
| Paint | Uniform color, even gaps | Mismatched, overspray, wide gaps |
| Warning lights | Only seatbelt on | Check engine, ABS, airbag |
| AC | Cold within 2–3 min | Never gets cold |
| Title | Clean, seller's name | Salvage, rebuilt, or different name |

### Private Party (Facebook Marketplace, OfferUp, Craigslist)

When buying from a private seller:
- Always meet in a public place (police station parking lot is ideal)
- Bring a friend
- Bring cash (but don't flash it)
- Get the VIN and run a check before you go
- Test drive with the seller in the car
- Do the paperwork at the MVD together

### Tips for Test Driving at a Dealer

1. **Call ahead** — Ask if they have the specific car you want on the lot
2. **Schedule a test drive** — Most dealers let you book online or by phone
3. **Bring your license** — They'll need it for the test drive
4. **Take your time** — Don't let them rush you
5. **Drive your normal route** — If possible, drive the roads you'll actually use
6. **Ask questions** — About the car's history, maintenance, any issues
7. **Get the VIN** — Run your own check even if they provide a report
8. **Negotiate** — Most local dealers expect it; CarMax and DriveTime don't
